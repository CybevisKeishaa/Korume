# Microcopy Guidelines

**Project:** Nihongo Cinema / AI Language OS  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Living Document

---

# Scope

This document defines **product-facing copy only** — how the product communicates through UI text.

It does not define:

- Companion personality (see `MASCOT.md`)
- AI behavior (see `ai-behavior-guidelines.md`)
- Product philosophy (see Product Philosophy / Spec 1)
- Experience mechanics (see `experience-architecture.md`)

---

# Purpose

Microcopy is part of the product experience, not decoration.

Every word should reduce friction, increase confidence, or quietly guide the learner forward.

The goal is never to sound "smart."

The goal is to make the learner feel:

> "This app understands what I'm trying to do."

---

# Core Philosophy

Microcopy should feel:

- calm
- human
- understated
- encouraging
- respectful
- emotionally intelligent

Never noisy.

Never sales-like.

Never manipulative.

---

# The Golden Rule

**Write as if you are sitting beside the learner, not standing in front of them.**

The product is a companion.

Not a coach yelling instructions.

Not a game trying to increase engagement.

Not an assistant trying to prove how intelligent it is.

---

# Tone

## Calm

Prefer:

> Ready when you are.

Instead of:

> Let's go!!

---

Prefer:

> You can continue anytime.

Instead of:

> Don't lose your streak!

---

## Quiet Confidence

The product should never sound uncertain.

But it should also never exaggerate.

Good:

> This sentence may be worth another listen.

Bad:

> You definitely don't understand this.

---

## Respectful

Never assume.

Never judge.

Never blame.

Avoid:

> Wrong.

Prefer:

> Almost there.

or

> Try listening once more.

---

# Speak Like a Human

Write naturally.

Not like UI generated by an AI.

Good:

> That expression appears often in casual conversation.

Bad:

> This lexical item exhibits high conversational frequency.

---

Good:

> You already know most of these words.

Bad:

> Vocabulary familiarity score: 84%.

---

# Prefer Observation Over Evaluation

The system should describe what happened.

Not judge it.

Good:

> You replayed this sentence a few times.

Better:

> It looks like this sentence needed a little more time.

Bad:

> You struggled here.

---

# Encourage Without Hype

The product should celebrate progress quietly.

Good:

> Nice.

Good:

> One more Beat finished.

Good:

> You're getting familiar with this pattern.

Avoid:

> Amazing!!

> Incredible!!

> You're unstoppable!!

Those words lose meaning very quickly.

---

# Don't Overuse Emotion

Emotion has value because it is rare.

If every screen says something emotional...

nothing feels emotional anymore.

Most screens should simply help.

Only a few moments deserve warmth.

Examples:

- finishing a difficult Beat
- completing a monthly Wrapped
- returning after a long break
- reaching a meaningful milestone

---

# Avoid Pressure

Never create anxiety.

Avoid:

> You still have 42 reviews waiting.

Prefer:

> A few reviews are ready whenever you are.

---

Avoid:

> You missed your goal.

Prefer:

> Tomorrow is another chance.

---

Avoid:

> Complete today's tasks.

Prefer:

> Continue where you left off.

---

# Never Guilt the Learner

Forbidden patterns:

- We miss you.
- Don't give up.
- Your streak is dying.
- Come back now.
- You haven't studied today.

Instead:

> Welcome back.

or

> Ready for another session?

Nothing more.

---

# Small Is Better

If a sentence can lose five words...

remove them.

Instead of:

> Your pronunciation has shown significant improvement compared to previous sessions.

Write:

> Your pronunciation is becoming more natural.

---

# Every Screen Has One Job

Do not explain multiple ideas in one sentence.

One screen.

One message.

One action.

---

# Explain Only When Needed

Don't teach the user everything immediately.

Progressive disclosure is better.

Instead of:

> Learning Beats divide videos into semantic learning units that reduce cognitive load.

Write:

> Beat 2 of 5

That's enough.

---

# Confidence Without Certainty

The system makes observations.

Not absolute truths.

Prefer:

> It seems...

> It looks like...

> You may find...

Avoid:

> We know...

> You always...

> You never...

---

# AI Should Stay Invisible

The product should rarely mention AI.

Bad:

> AI analyzed your learning style.

Better:

> We've prepared a review for you.

Best:

> Ready to review?

Users care about outcomes.

Not technology.

---

# Companion Voice

Companion speaks differently from the product.

The product gives information.

The Companion speaks only when something genuinely matters.

Companion messages should feel personal.

Never frequent.

Never repetitive.

Never motivational for its own sake.

---

Good Companion Lines

> That sentence was difficult.

> You stayed with it.

---

> You came back.

> I'm glad you did.

---

> Some conversations take time.

> This one is starting to feel familiar.

---

> You've seen this expression before.

> It feels a little more natural now.

---

# Forbidden Companion Lines

Never say:

> Great job!

> Keep it up!

> You can do it!

> Believe in yourself!

> You're amazing!

These could belong to any app.

The Companion should only speak from shared history.

---

# Data Before Emotion

Every personal sentence should be grounded in real learning data.

Good:

> You no longer replay this scene.

Bad:

> You're becoming confident.

---

Good:

> This expression used to slow you down.

Bad:

> You're improving every day.

Specific observations build trust.

Generic praise does not.

---

# Empty States

Empty states should invite.

Not apologize.

Good:

> Your memories will appear here.

---

Good:

> Nothing to review yet.

---

Good:

> Your journey starts with one video.

Avoid:

> No data available.

---

# Error Messages

Errors should explain:

- what happened
- what the user can do next

Never blame.

Bad:

> Invalid input.

Better:

> We couldn't process this video.

Try another link, or try again later.

---

# Success Messages

Keep them small.

Good:

> Saved.

Good:

> Added to your playlist.

Good:

> Review updated.

No celebration unless it truly deserves one.

---

# Loading States

Loading text should reassure.

Good:

> Preparing your lesson...

> Analyzing the conversation...

> Building your review...

Avoid fake progress.

Avoid jokes.

Avoid random tips that interrupt the mood.

---

# Notifications

Every notification must answer:

**Why should the learner care right now?**

If the answer isn't clear...

don't send it.

---

# Silence Is Also Microcopy

Sometimes the best text is no text.

No popup.

No tooltip.

No celebration.

No reminder.

Silence creates room for focus.

Microcopy should know when to disappear.

---

# Writing Checklist

Before shipping any sentence, ask:

- Does it reduce friction?
- Does it respect the learner?
- Is it grounded in real behavior?
- Can it be shorter?
- Would this still sound natural after the 100th time?
- Is this the right moment to say anything at all?

If the answer to the last question is "no"...

delete the sentence.

---

# One Final Principle

The learner is the protagonist.

The product is the environment.

The Companion is a quiet presence.

Microcopy exists to make learning feel lighter—

never louder.
